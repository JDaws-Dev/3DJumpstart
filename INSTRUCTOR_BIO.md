## Long Version (For Detailed About Page)

### Background

Hi, I'm Jeremiah Daws, and I've had a pretty unconventional path to teaching 3D design.

I started my career as a video producer, working for Disney in both LA and Orlando. During that time, I ran a machining business on the side, designing and fabricating custom equipment used in Marvel film productions. I have a Film degree from Georgia State University and a Machining degree from Lanier Technical College—a weird combination that turned out to be perfect for what I do now.

### How I Got Into 3D Design & Making

During the pandemic, I dove headfirst into the maker world. I taught myself 3D modeling, advanced machining, 3D printing, welding, electronics—basically any skill that let me turn ideas into real objects. What started as a hobby became an obsession, and I realized I had found my calling: helping students discover the same joy of creating.

### Teaching at Artios Academies

I currently teach Film and Creative Technology at Artios Academies in Sugar Hill, where I pioneered our 3D design curriculum. Over 50 students have learned CAD modeling, precision measuring, and how to operate 3D printers through my program.

Seeing these students go from "I have no idea how this works" to confidently designing and printing their own creations—that's what drives me.

### Why 3D Jumpstart?

I saw how transformative this curriculum was for my Artios students, and I wanted to open it up to more kids in the North Gwinnett area.

Too many students are stuck in programs that teach "toy" software or treat 3D printing like magic. I teach them **professional skills**—the same CAD software used by NASA and Tesla engineers. I want every student to experience the pride and satisfaction of holding something they designed and built themselves.

### Teaching Philosophy

Every student learns differently. I've taught kids who pick up CAD in 20 minutes and kids who need three classes to understand the first concept. Both are equally capable—they just need different approaches.

My job is to figure out what makes each student tick and adapt my teaching style accordingly. Some kids need visual demonstrations. Some need to jump in and tinker. Some need to understand the "why" before they can do the "how." I meet them where they are.

The best part of teaching? When students choose to stay late or spend their free time working on projects. When they're personally motivated—not because it's required, but because they genuinely want to create—that's when real learning happens.

### Beyond the Classroom

I'm a Gwinnett County native who spent time away (Disney in LA and Orlando) but came back home because I love it here. I'm the "ultimate fun uncle" to my 9 nieces and nephews—I even built them a pirate-themed treehouse (designed in Fusion360, naturally).

I also make props for the Artios theater department, do custom fabrication projects, and basically spend all my free time building things. Making is my love language.

### What I Want for Your Student

I want your child to:
1. Learn **real, professional skills** that colleges and employers value
2. Discover the confidence that comes from creating something tangible
3. Develop problem-solving skills that apply far beyond CAD
4. Find a new way to express themselves through design
5. Maybe catch the maker bug and never look back

If your child finishes my class and says "I want to learn more about this," I've done my job.

Let's build something amazing together.

—Jeremiah
